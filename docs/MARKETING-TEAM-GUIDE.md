# Marketing Scholars: guide for the marketing team

**One line:** Reverse-engineer your competitors' marketing strategies. Then build yours.

**Live site:** https://reverse-engineering-marketing.vercel.app (moving to our Marketing Scholars domain)

This guide covers what the product does, how to talk about it, what content to make, and how to run A/B tests on messaging. It is written for marketers. No coding is needed except where marked **[dev]**.

---

## 1. What the product does

A visitor pastes a competitor's website address. In under a minute, Marketing Scholars reads that competitor's public pages and emails their marketing strategy as a well-designed PDF. The report goes by email only: the visitor confirms their email first (one click), then the PDF arrives within about two minutes. Paid plans arrive right after payment. So every report is a confirmed email lead. Use "check your inbox" in copy, not "download".

| | Free teardown | Full plan |
|---|---|---|
| **Input** | Competitor URL and email | Competitor URL, then one of two paths: **I have a website** (brand name and domain) or **Starting fresh** (what you sell and who to; name, city and budget optional) |
| **Output** | ~9-page PDF of their strategy | ~22-page PDF: their strategy plus your plan |
| **Price** | Free | ₹499 one-time via UPI (free during launch week) |

**Inside the free teardown** (10 sections):
1. Executive summary
2. Positioning: one-liner, audience, awareness stage, core offer and main CTA
3. Audience: segments, the pains they speak to, and the objections they handle
4. Channel map: which channels are active, likely or absent, with evidence
5. Content engine: cadence, formats and CTA pattern
6. SEO footprint: page types and programmatic page patterns (for example 400+ `/integrations/` pages)
7. Funnel and offer: entry offer, pricing model and what's behind the paywall
8. What works for them, and whether it transfers to you
9. Their blind spots
10. Quick wins for a smaller rival

Every PDF opens with a clickable contents page: tap a section to jump to it.

**The full plan adds:**
- *I have a website:* "Where you stand today" and a "You vs them" table of 14 things we checked on both sites, each marked ahead, even or behind, with the fix
- *Starting fresh:* a launch kit with name ideas (if no name yet), the pages to build, a homepage outline and a launch checklist; the first 30 days focus on launch foundations
- positioning statement, differentiators and taglines
- ideal customer and where they gather
- 4–6 strategies built on what works for the competitor
- channel plan
- 30/60/90-day plan with hours and stop rules
- 12-week content calendar
- SEO keywords, ad angles and a 6-email welcome sequence
- swipe file, KPIs and risks

### How it works, in plain words
The tool visits the competitor's homepage, pricing page and blog, plus three files websites publish for search engines: `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml` and `llms.txt`. It detects 50+ marketing tools on the site, including ad pixels (Meta, Google, LinkedIn, TikTok), email and CRM tools (HubSpot, Klaviyo and others), analytics and chat. It counts pages by type and spots repeated page templates, then AI turns these signals into a strategy.

### What it does NOT do (never claim these)
- **No traffic or ad-spend numbers.** These can't be seen from outside, and the report says so.
- **No ad library or social engagement data yet.** It shows which pixels and profiles exist, not how ads perform.
- **No logged-in or private pages.** Public pages only.
- **Not every site works.** Some block automated readers, and sites built entirely in JavaScript give thinner results. The advice is always: run the free teardown first.
- **Never copying.** The report teaches the competitor's *mechanism*, never to copy their words, images or brand. This is a promise, so keep it in our messaging.

- **Words to use:** study, playbook, reverse-engineer, build yours, blind spots, what works, mechanism.
- **Words to avoid:** spy, hack, steal, copy (except in "copy the mechanism, never the material"), revolutionary, game-changing, guaranteed results.

## 4. A/B testing with messaging

### What's running now on the website
Every visitor is randomly placed in one version of each test and always sees the same versions when they come back.

| Test | Version A | Version B |
|---|---|---|
| **Positioning** (headline) | "Reverse-engineer your competitors' marketing strategies. **Then build yours.**" | "Your competitor's marketing strategy, reverse-engineered. **Your plan, built from it.**" |
| **Opener** (kicker + paragraph under the headline) | **Founder:** "18 years of brand building, in one tool. Every brand transformation starts the same way: studying what already works…" | **Curiosity:** "Every website leaks its marketing. The pixels it fires, the pages it builds…" |

The design test is finished: the light "editorial" design won and everyone sees it.

### See any version yourself
Add these to the site address:
- `?pos=a&hook=a`: headline A, founder opener
- `?pos=b&hook=b`: headline B, curiosity opener
- `?ab=reset`: get a new random version

### What we measure

| Step | Event name | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Visit | `lp_view` | Someone landed on the page |
| Intent | `cta_click` | Clicked a button (records which one) |
| Started | `report_started` | Submitted a competitor URL |
| **Got value** | **`report_completed`** | **Received a teardown. This is our main success metric.** |
| Asked | `report_requested`, `confirm_email_sent` | Submitted the form; confirmation email sent |
| Confirmed | `email_confirmed` | Clicked the link; the report is being emailed |
| Used it | `pdf_download`, `html_view` | Downloaded or viewed the report (only before email-only delivery was switched on) |
| Upgrade | `upgrade_click`, `checkout_started`, `payment_success` | Moved toward and through payment |

Every event is tagged with the visitor's versions (`exp_pos`, `exp_hook`) and their `utm_source` / `utm_campaign`. **[dev]** Analytics needs the free PostHog key set in Vercel; see `docs/LAUNCH.md`.

### How to read results (in PostHog)
1. Go to **Insights → New → Funnel**, with steps `lp_view` → `report_started` → `report_completed`.
2. Set **Breakdown** to `exp_pos` for the headline test. Duplicate it with `exp_hook` for the opener test.
3. Compare the conversion rate from `lp_view` to `report_completed` per version.

### When is a result real?
- **Wait for about 3,800 visitors per version** before deciding. That's enough to spot a jump from 10% to 12%.
- With both tests running together, about 7,600 total visitors answers both.
- Don't stop early because one version "looks" ahead after a few hundred visits. Early leads often flip.
- A winner must win on `report_completed`, not just clicks.
- Once there's a winner, the team switches every visitor to it and starts the next test in its slot. **[dev]**

### How to propose a new test
Copy this template into the team doc:

```
Test name:
Slot: headline (pos) / opener (hook) / new slot [dev]
Hypothesis: If we change ___ to ___, more visitors will ___ because ___.
Version A (current):
Version B (new):
Primary metric: report_completed / lp_view
Audience/traffic source:
Owner:            Start date:            Decision date:
```

Rules for good tests:
1. **One idea per test.** Changing the headline and the button in one test means you won't know which one worked.
2. **Make the versions really different.** "Get a plan" vs "Get your plan" teaches nothing; outcome vs curiosity does.
3. **Keep promises consistent.** An ad's message must match the page it lands on.
4. **Write down the result** in the test log below, including losers. Losers teach too.

### Test backlog (suggested order)

| # | Slot | Idea | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Button text | "Reverse-engineer a competitor free" vs "Show me their strategy" | Button copy often moves conversion cheaply |
| 2 | Form | URL only (email asked after the report) vs URL + email upfront | Friction vs lead capture trade-off |
| 3 | Hero visual | Scanner animation vs a 30-second demo video | Proof of mechanism |
| 4 | Social proof | Founder photo + one line under the form vs none | Trust at the moment of action |
| 5 | Price anchor | "₹499 (an agency audit costs weeks)" vs plain "₹499" | Value framing (once paid) |
| 6 | Opener | Fear: "Your competitor is studying you" vs founder opener | Emotional trigger |
| 7 | Upsell timing | Show the full-plan offer before vs after the report | Upgrade rate |
| 8 | Launch bar | "Launch week: full plan free" vs no bar | Urgency effect |

### Test log (keep updating)

| Test | Dates | Visitors per version | A result | B result | Winner | What we learned |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Design: dark vs editorial | launch | review | | | Editorial | Lighter, bolder design preferred |
| Positioning | | | | | | |
| Opener | | | | | | |

### Testing messaging outside the website (fast and cheap)
Before changing the site, test messages where feedback is quick:
1. **LinkedIn:** post the same idea with two different hooks on different days. Compare saves and comments per 1,000 impressions.
2. **Ads:** with ₹2,000–5,000 per test, run two ad versions with the same image and different first lines for 3–5 days. Send both to the site with `utm_content=hookA` / `hookB`.
3. **Email subject lines:** split the list 50/50, then compare open and click rates.

Winning lines graduate into the website tests.
